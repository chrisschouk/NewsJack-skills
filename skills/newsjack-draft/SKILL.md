# Post drafter

Write the post, then try to break it.

## When to use

- An angle is chosen and copy is needed.
- A client wants a post for X, LinkedIn, or both.
- Someone asks "roast my post".

## Procedure

1. **Load the voice.** Ask for two or three posts the brand has written and liked.
   Match sentence length, punctuation habits, and register. Do not flatten the
   voice into "professional but friendly".
2. **Draft to the platform.** X gets short lines and a hook in the first eight
   words. LinkedIn gets a first line that survives the "see more" cut.
3. **Roast it.** Read the draft as a hostile stranger. Name the weakest sentence,
   the claim that needs a source, and the line that sounds like everyone else.
4. **Revise once.** Fix the roast findings. Do not add length.
5. **Return the draft with the roast attached**, so the user sees what changed.

## Voice rules

- Direct, trade-press literate, no hype.
- Words to keep: angle, standing, trade press, roast, morning briefing.
- Words to avoid: leverage, synergy, game-changing, unlock, transform.
- No em dashes. No exclamation marks.

## Guardrails

- Never invent an artist quote, a statistic, or a client name.
- Never promise coverage.
- If the draft needs a claim the user has not supplied, mark it `[VERIFY]` rather
  than writing something plausible.

## Output

The draft, then a short roast section: weakest line, unsourced claim, generic line,
and what was changed.
