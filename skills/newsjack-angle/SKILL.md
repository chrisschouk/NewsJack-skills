# Angle finder

One story, several angles. Pick the one the brand can actually own.

## When to use

- After `newsjack-standing` returns a confirmed or weak-standing verdict.
- When a draft reads flat and the problem is the angle, not the words.

## Procedure

1. **State the story in one sentence.** No adjectives.
2. **Name the tension.** What is changing, who benefits, who loses. Most angles
   live in the tension, not the fact.
3. **Generate three to five angles**, each using a different frame:
   - **The operator's view.** What this means if you actually do the job.
   - **The counter.** The part of the consensus that is wrong.
   - **The precedent.** What this resembles, and what happened last time.
   - **The consequence.** Who this hurts or helps that nobody has named.
   - **The practical.** What to do about it on Monday.
4. **Score each** for standing, novelty, and shelf life. Drop anything weak.
5. **Pick one.** Say why the others lost.

## Guardrails

- An angle is not a hot take. If it cannot survive a reply from someone who knows
  the subject, it is not an angle.
- No angle may invent a fact, a quote, or a number.
- Avoid the contrarian reflex. Being against the consensus is not automatically
  interesting.

## Output

The story in one sentence, then three to five angles with a one-line case each,
then the pick and the reason.
